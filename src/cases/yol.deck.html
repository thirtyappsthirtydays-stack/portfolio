02 · Define

Designing for Elif

Elif Arslan, 36Product lead · lives in Moda, works in Levent

“Tell me what I’ll see at the turn, and what to do if I can’t make it.”

Problem statementElif needs directions she can trust on streets that change faster than the map, because near the Bosphorus one wrong turn can commit her to the wrong crossing.
How might weMake the app honest about what it doesn’t know, and ready for the moment the map is wrong?

Six principles

  • 01Use local words and landmarks
  • 02Say when the app is unsure
  • 03Plan B before the junction
  • 04No tapping while moving
  • 05Flag choices that can’t be undone
  • 06Private by default

Proto-persona. Stock photo, not a real participant.

03 · Develop

Three ways to handle a doubtful turn

The hardest moment to design. I sketched three behaviours and judged each against the principles.

Option A · Breaks 02 and 03Stay silent, reroute afterShe finds out at the junction
Option B · Breaks 04Ask the driverA question at speed is a distraction
Option C · Chosen · Meets 02, 03 and 04Show the evidence, prepare Plan BSays the turn is in doubt, shows why, and has a fallback ready

Four confidence levels, one behaviour each

  • 1Sure · one instruction, the sign and the lanes
  • 2Unsure where you are · both instructions, by road
  • 3Doubtful turn · an amber chip, the evidence and Plan B
  • 4Confirmed closed · the turn leaves the map

One reading order on every driving screen: what to do, how sure, why, where, what if, when.

Results

What success would look like

This is a concept, so these are targets for a three-month pilot on a few Istanbul corridors.

What workedShowing both instructions when unsureBoth instructions, or the evidence behind a doubt, made the app’s uncertain moments clear to drivers.

What I’d test first: are two instructions calm, or confusing? In a parked car, phone mounted.
