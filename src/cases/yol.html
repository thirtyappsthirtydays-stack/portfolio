What I found

Insight 1

The street changes faster than the map

New medians and banned turns reach the map late, and the app repeats the old instruction with full confidence.

Insight 2

Drivers navigate by signs and landmarks

Overhead signs list districts, like Levent · Maslak, and locals give directions by landmarks.

Insight 3

GPS mixes up parallel roads

Main and service roads (yan yol) run side by side, and GPS can’t always tell which one you’re on.

Insight 4

Some mistakes can’t be undone

Miss the exit near the Bosphorus and you may be committed to a crossing, with its toll.

02 · Define

Designing for Elif

I combined the research into one persona: a commuter who crosses the Bosphorus twice a day.

Proto-personaElif Arslan, 36Product lead · lives in Moda, works in Levent

“Tell me what I’ll see at the turn, and what to do if I can’t make it.”

Goals

  • Arrive on time, with no surprises at junctions
  • Know her options before a choice becomes irreversible

Frustrations

  • Instructions the street won’t allow
  • “Rerouting” in the middle of a junction

Proto-persona. Stock photo, not a real participant.

Problem statement

Elif needs directions she can trust on streets that change faster than the map, because near the Bosphorus one wrong turn can commit her to the wrong crossing.

How might we

Make the app honest about what it doesn’t know, and ready for the moment the map is wrong?

Six principles

01

Use local words and landmarks

The words on the sign and the landmarks locals use.

02

Say when the app is unsure

When the app doesn’t know, it says so and shows why.

03

Plan B before the junction

A fallback is ready before a risky turn.

04

No tapping while moving

Voice in the moment, questions after parking.

05

Flag choices that can’t be undone

Name the last point where a choice can still change.

06

Private by default

Reports are anonymous and location history isn’t kept.
