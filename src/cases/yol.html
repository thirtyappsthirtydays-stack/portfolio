Where directions break in Istanbul

Istanbul’s roads change faster than map data, and its layout punishes small mistakes: service roads beside main roads, exits close together, concrete medians, and a strait you can only cross at a few points.

What I found

Insight 1

The street changes faster than the map

New medians, banned turns and one-way changes reach the map late. Until then, the app repeats an old instruction with full confidence.

Insight 2

Drivers read signs, not street names

Overhead signs list districts, like Levent · Maslak, and locals give directions by landmarks.

Insight 3

Two roads, one blue dot

Main roads and service roads (yan yol) run side by side. GPS can’t always tell which one you’re on, so the instructions jump too.

Insight 4

Some mistakes can’t be undone

Miss the right exit near the Bosphorus and you may be committed to a crossing, with its toll. The app only reacts after the mistake.

02 · Define

Designing for Elif

I brought the research together into one primary persona, then turned her commute into a problem statement and six principles to test every design against.

Problem statement

Elif needs directions she can trust on streets that change faster than the map, because near the Bosphorus one wrong turn can commit her to the wrong crossing.

How might we

Make the app honest about what it doesn’t know, and ready for the moment the map is wrong?

Proto-personaElif Arslan, 36Product lead · lives in Moda, works in Levent

“Don’t just tell me where to turn. Tell me what I’ll see, and what to do if I can’t.”

Drives
Four days a week, about 40 minutes each way
Crosses
The Bosphorus twice a day
Relies on
Navigation for traffic; signs and landmarks for directions

Goals

  • Arrive on time, with no surprises at junctions
  • Know her options before a choice becomes irreversible
  • Keep her eyes and hands on the road

Frustrations

  • Instructions the street won’t allow
  • “Rerouting” in the middle of a junction
  • No easy way to say what was wrong

Proto-persona from desk research, to validate in interviews. Stock photo, not a real participant.

How I’d measure it
  • Reroutes after an instruction the street didn’t allow
  • Blocked turns handled by a prepared Plan B
  • Drivers who answer the post-trip questions
  • Days from first report to a fixed map

Six principles

01

Speak like the street

Use the words on the sign and the landmarks locals use. Talk like a friend in the passenger seat.

02

Never fake certainty

When the app doesn’t know where you are, or whether a turn exists, it says so and shows why.

03

Plan B before the junction

A fallback is ready before a risky turn, so a blocked turn is a non-event, not a panic.

04

No tapping while moving

Voice in the moment, questions after parking. Nothing asks for attention a driver doesn’t have.

05

Respect the irreversible

Name the last point where a choice can still change, like which Bosphorus crossing to take.

06

Private by default

Reports are anonymous and location history isn’t kept. Helping the map costs the driver nothing.

03 · Develop

Three ways to handle a doubtful turn

The uncertain turn was the hardest moment to design. I sketched three behaviours as low-fidelity screens and judged each against the principles, then made the same kind of call for every other moment.

Option A

Stay silent, reroute after

Today’s behaviour. The driver finds out at the junction, with cars behind her.

Option B

Ask the driver

Visual language and Turkish

Blue is the way, amber is doubt

Two colours carry all the meaning, eight components are learned once, and every string was written in Turkish first, not translated into it.

Everything else stays neutral

So a glance finds the route and the doubt first. Driving screens are dark so they never outshine the road; planning and review screens are light, because the car is parked.

Designing in Turkish, not translating into it

Several details change how strings have to be built, not only how they read, so each comes with a note for engineering.

Zorlu’dan · Zorlu Center’ın · Gonca Sokak’aCase endings follow the name, and how it’s said: “Center” is said sentır, so it takes -’ın.Build: store suffix forms with each place. Never build “{place}’a”.
BELİRSİZ DÖNÜŞ · B PLANIUppercasing “belirsiz” with an English locale gives BELIRSIZ, a different word.Build: uppercase with tr-TR, never the default.
