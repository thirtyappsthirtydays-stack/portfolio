01 · Discover

Ready to invest, unsure how to start

Wealth management is shifting to digital-first investing. Plenty of people want to begin, but the step from “I’m saving for retirement” to an actual portfolio is where they stop.

Business context

Robo-advisors emerged to close that gap by recommending a portfolio from an investor’s profile. Citi Wealth Builder was Citi’s answer, built on Citi Investment Management’s portfolios.

1 · Define a goal2 · A short risk questionnaire3 · A matched portfolio, then open and fund

Wealth Builder Plus added access to a Financial Advisor for investors who wanted a person involved in bigger decisions.

Uncertain allocation
How much in stocks, how much in bonds, and why
Limited knowledge
Risk tolerance and asset mix mean little at first
Complex products
Funds, styles and fees, with no obvious place to begin

What I learned

From available research, market insights and Citi’s cross-segment customer data.

01

The goal is clear, the portfolio isn’t

People can say what they’re investing for. They can’t say what to buy for it.

02

Jargon ends it early

New investors open an app, meet unfamiliar terms and close it without investing.

03

Black boxes lose trust

Experienced investors want to see what’s inside a portfolio and what it costs.

04

Fear of a costly mistake

The closer the goal, the more people worry about getting risk wrong.

Core insight

Options aren’t the problem. Confidence is.

Investors rarely struggle with access to investment options. They struggle with decision confidence.

02 · Define

Three investors, one onboarding

From research, market insight and Citi’s customer data, the team built personas across goals, life stages, risk tolerance and experience. Three framed the spectrum, from a sceptical expert to someone with $50 and no idea where to start.

Problem statement

New investors know what they’re investing for, but not how to turn that goal and their comfort with risk into a portfolio they trust.

How might we

Help investors translate their financial goals and risk tolerance into a clear investment strategy, within a digital platform?

Onboarding completion
More people reach a strategy they’re happy with
Account activation
More of them open and fund the account
Decision confidence
They feel sure about the portfolio they chose
The Starter · newJordan, 26Graphic designer · Austin

“I have $50 to start and no idea what I’m doing. I just know I’m already behind.”

Needs
Guided onboarding, low minimums, simple goals, mobile first
Pain point
Jargon and fear of loss. Three apps opened and closed without investing a cent.

Onboarding must speak plainly, ask one thing at a time, and never feel like a test.

The Optimizer · advancedAlex, 34Software engineer · San Francisco

“I can build my own spreadsheet for this. Convince me a robo-advisor is actually worth it.”

Needs
Performance detail, low expense ratios, a way to inspect the strategy
Pain point
Robo-advisors feel like a black box, and fees quietly eat returns

Onboarding must show what’s inside: the asset mix, the holdings and how it compares.

The Strategist · seasonedMargaret, 52Marketing director · Chicago

“I just want something I can trust to handle this while I focus on everything else in my life.”

Needs
Set-and-forget investing, rebalancing, goal tracking, an advisor for big decisions
Pain point
Afraid of a costly mistake near retirement

Onboarding must recommend with confidence, make the risk visible, and offer a person.

Proto-personas from research synthesis and customer data. Stock photos, not real people.

Guidance, with the final say left to the investor

Each direction served one persona well and failed another, until the third balanced control with decision support.

Option 1

Self-service portfolio builder

Investors pick funds and allocations, supported by education.

Not chosenFull control for Alex, but it hands the hardest decision to people like Jordan.
Option 2

Fully automated portfolios

Minimal input, optimised on risk tolerance and time horizon.

Not chosenThe fastest path, but the black box Alex distrusts, and Margaret never sees her risk.
Option 3

A guided investment journey

Goals, risk profiling and a recommendation, with the investor in control at key points.

ChosenEach persona gets help where they need it, and a choice where they want one.
01

Simplify complex concepts

Ask about life and feelings, not finance terms. Translate into jargon behind the scenes.

02

Guide decisions step by step

One decision per screen, in the order people think: why, how, then what.

03

Insight without overwhelm

Detail is one tap away for Alex, and out of the way until Jordan is ready.

04

Recommend, don’t decide

Lead with a Best Match, show the alternatives beside it. The investor makes the call.

03 · Develop

Structure first, then a need for every screen

Before any colour, each step was a greyscale wireframe with one job, so feedback stayed on order and wording. Then each screen answered one need from research, without a single finance term in the questions.

Make the Best Match obvious, without hiding the other two

On desktop the strategy step has room to show all three portfolios side by side.

04 · Deliver

The promise, before the first question

Onboarding starts on the guided investing page. It answers what stops people at the door, what it costs, what they need and what happens next, before anyone is asked anything.

After onboarding, the plan made visible

Investors see what they own, what it could grow into, and what a different strategy would have meant.

Results and reflection

Strong early adoption

After launch, Wealth Builder showed strong early adoption and positive feedback, suggesting onboarding had cleared the barrier it was built for.

What I learnedBehavioural design matters in financePeople rarely lack options. They lack confidence in a decision. One step at a time turned an intimidating process into an approachable one.
Working across teamsDesign as the bridgeInvestment Strategy’s models into plain questions, release scope with product, one flow on web and iOS with engineering, and disclosures with Compliance and Legal.
What I’d explore nextDeeper personalisationPortfolios beyond three strategies, proactive insights when a plan drifts from its goal, and AI recommendations that still leave the final say with the investor.
