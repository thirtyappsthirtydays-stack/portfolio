02 · Define

Designing for three kinds of investor

Three personas framed how much help each investor wanted, from someone with savings and no idea where to start to an investor nearing retirement.

The Starter · newJillian, 26Graphic designer · Austin

“I’ve saved $30,000 and it’s just sitting in my checking account. I want someone to tell me I’m doing this right.”

Needs
Guidance, a person to ask, mobile first
Pain point
Jargon and fear of loss

Onboarding must speak plainly, one thing at a time, with an advisor to ask.

The Optimizer · advancedAlex, 34Software engineer · San Francisco

“I can build my own spreadsheet for this. Show me what an advisor adds before I pay for one.”

Needs
Performance detail and a clear fee
Pain point
Managed portfolios feel like a black box

Onboarding must show what’s inside and what the fee pays for.

The Strategist · seasonedMargaret, 52Marketing director · Chicago

“I just want something I can trust to handle this while I focus on everything else in my life.”

Needs
Set-and-forget, and an advisor for big decisions
Pain point
A costly mistake near retirement

Onboarding must recommend with confidence and offer a person.

Problem statement

Investors know what they’re investing for, but not how to turn that goal and their comfort with risk into a portfolio they trust. Many want a person to check it with, and full advisory costs more than they want to pay.

How might we

Help investors turn their goals and risk tolerance into a managed portfolio, with a Financial Advisor alongside, at a lower cost than full advisory?

A guided journey, with an advisor built in

Three directions, tested against the three personas.

Option 1

Self-service portfolio builder

Investors pick funds, with education.

Not chosenSelf Invest already does this, and it hands the hardest decision to Jillian.
Option 2

Fully automated portfolios

Minimal input, no person involved.

Not chosenWealth Builder already does this, with no one for Margaret to ask.
Option 3

A guided journey with an advisor

Goals, risk and a recommendation, then time with a Financial Advisor.

ChosenHelp where each persona needs it.

What I learnedBehavioural design matters in financeConfidence to decide was the barrier. One step at a time, with a person to ask, made an intimidating process approachable.
Working across teamsTranslating between teamsInvestment Strategy’s models into plain questions, scope with product, one flow with engineering, disclosures with Compliance and Legal.
