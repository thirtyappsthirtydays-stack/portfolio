01 · Discover

Plenty of information, no way to narrow it

Modern investment platforms offer thousands of ETFs and mutual funds. Without good filtering, a self-directed investor has to research them one by one, and that friction sits right at the start of building a portfolio.

Business context

For a wealth platform like Citi, better discovery tools can raise investor confidence, encourage more portfolio activity and lift the whole digital wealth experience. The Screener was part of Citi Self Invest, Citi’s platform for self-directed investors.

What I learned

From observing the existing product and 3 rounds of user interviews with 15 investors.

01

Too many options

Thousands of funds, and no quick way to rule most of them out.

02

Unclear criteria

Terms like equity style or target asset mix meant little without an explanation.

03

Hard to compare

Weighing fees against returns across several funds meant a lot of back and forth.

04

No guidance

Newer investors didn’t know where to start, or which filter to pick first.

Core insight

Investors weren’t short of information.

They were short of a way to narrow thousands of funds down to the few that fit.

02 · Define

Two investors, one screener

The research pointed to two ends of the same audience: people new to funds who needed a place to start, and experienced investors who wanted full control. The Screener had to work for both without slowing either down.

Problem statement

Self-directed investors need to narrow thousands of funds to the few that match their goals, quickly and with confidence, instead of researching funds one by one.

How might we

Give investors control over the whole fund universe, and a clear place to start?

Four principles

01

Narrow thousands, fast

Every choice cuts the list right away and shows how many funds are left.

02

Criteria you can see and understand

Active filters stay visible, and every category says what it means.

03

Compare on the same measures

Price, cost and returns line up in columns across every fund.

04

Guidance you can skip

Help is offered at the start and one click away after, never forced.

Control first, then a model to hold it

We weighed three product directions. The one we chose put investors in charge of their own criteria, so the next job was a filter model they could understand at a glance.

Option 1

Redesign the fund catalogue

Better hierarchy and progressive disclosure on the existing list.

Not chosenEasier to read, but still a list to scroll. Investors couldn’t say what they were looking for.
Option 2

AI-driven recommendations

Suggest funds from investor profiles and behavioural signals.

Parked for laterLess effort, but it takes the decision away from people who chose a self-directed product.
Option 3

A flexible screener

Investors define their own criteria and compare funds side by side.

ChosenIt fits self-directed investors, who want control over their investment decisions.
The filter model

11 filters in 3 groups, chosen by researching which categories mattered most

Sort by
Performance, highest first, or expense ratio, lowest first

03 · Develop

From user need to design solution

I worked in greyscale so feedback stayed on structure and wording, not colour. Each screen answers one user need from research.

NeedIntuitive filter designSolutionPills that show every active filter

A pill for each active group, right above the results it shapes. The × removes it in one click, the matching count and data timestamp sit beside them, and Clear Filters starts over.

NeedThe ability to buy fundsSolutionAn actions menu on every row

One quiet trigger per row, so the row stays about the numbers. Buy and Sell are the only buttons. Auto Invest and Watchlist sit in the same menu, so nobody loses the screen.

Need · Clear onboardingTwo ways in for newer investors, both skippable

A short tour for people who’d rather explore, and two questions for people who want a starting point.

Design system

New patterns, inside an existing system

As one of the designers maintaining Citi’s design system and component library, I fitted the Screener’s new patterns into it instead of inventing a new look. Two blues carry the whole product.

Blue to act, dark blue for structure

Citi Blue appears only on chips, pills, checkboxes and the Buy, Sell and Apply buttons. Everything else stays neutral, so the eye goes to choices and actions.
