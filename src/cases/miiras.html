01 · Discover

Why families keep putting it off

My initial idea was that families needed an app that stored all of their wealth information in one place to prepare for the great wealth transfer. Before designing anything, I interviewed both generations.

What I learned

Insight 1

Nobody wants to be the one to bring it up

Both generations said it mattered, and each waited for the other to raise it.

Insight 2

Families want to know they’re prepared

Their biggest worry was whether everyone would be okay if something changed.

Insight 3

Small questions are easier to start with

One easy question over dinner usually got an answer.

Insight 4 · The pivot

Families needed help starting the conversation

My first concept tracked what a family owns, which a family that has never talked about money can’t fill in.

02 · Define

Designing for Talya

I combined the research into one persona: the family member who decides it’s time to talk.

Proto-personaTalya, 36Marketing manager · lives a flight away from her parents

“I want to know they’ll be okay, without it sounding like I’m asking about the will.”

Goals

  • Know her parents’ wishes before a crisis
  • Bring her brother into the same conversation

Frustrations

  • Every attempt turns into a change of subject
  • Worries she’ll sound like she’s after something

Proto-persona from research synthesis. Stock photo, not a real participant.

Problem statement

Talya needs a gentle way to start talking with her parents about money, care and their wishes, because waiting for the right moment means the talk happens in a crisis, or not at all.

How might we

Make the first question small enough to ask tonight, and the next one easy to follow?

Four principles

01

Small first, deeper later

Every deck opens gently and goes deeper card by card.

02

No numbers needed

Nobody has to share an amount to take part.

03

What’s said stays at the table

No account, no recordings and no saved answers.

04

Calm and practical

Calm language and no scores.

03 · Develop

Two concepts, tested, then distilled

I tested two clickable concepts before building, then simplified the preferred one into cards.

Concept A · 6 screens

Weekly questions

One question a week, with a place to log the answer.

Not taken forwardParticipants preferred the guided plan.
Concept B · 8 screens

A worry-led conversation plan

Start from a worry and follow a step-by-step plan.

Preferred in testingIts small-to-deep order carried into the final design.
Final direction

Conversation cards

The plan’s order, as decks a family reads aloud together.

What I cut to shipThe AI practice partner, recordings and saved answers, for privacy and simplicity.

Usability testing

3 rounds, 5 participants each

Round 130%task completion
After round 1

Showed one card at a time instead of a list of questions, because people scrolled and skimmed.

After round 2

Added progress dots and a clear end, so pairs knew when they were done.

Round 350%task completion

The question comes first on every card

The card is read aloud, often by someone holding the phone for others, so the question has to stand out.

  • The questionFraunces 30/36, the largest type on screen
  • One filled buttonNext is the only navy fill. Swiping works too.
  • Who it’s for, and how to askA white pill on all five tints, with a tip underneath
  • Context and progressArea and level in small caps, and ten progress segments

Design system

Every value is the one the app ships with
