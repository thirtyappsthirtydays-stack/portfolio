What I learned

Insight 1

The person booking is often not the patient

Children book for parents, parents for babies. The first form asked “What is your name?”. After review, every question asks about the patient, and the phone number can belong to a relative.

Insight 2

WhatsApp is the front door

Patients already message the practice on WhatsApp, and the surgeon prefers WhatsApp video. Not everyone has it, so the flow needs a Google Meet fallback.

Insight 3

The records are the consultation

CT, PET-CT, lab and pathology reports must reach him early enough to read. Imaging files are large, and most records already sit in E-Nabız, Turkey’s national health portal.

Insight 4

The money has to land in a Turkish account

Fees paid upfront, into the practice’s account. Bank transfer is familiar, free and needs no integration, but nothing confirms it automatically.

02 · Define

Two people, one booking

Bookings are often made around the patient rather than by her, and every booking lands in the surgeon’s week. I wrote a persona for each side, then one problem statement and four principles that name who they protect.

Problem statement

Ayten’s family needs a clear, trusted way to book Prof. Halezeroğlu and get her records to him in time, because a surgical decision is waiting on his answer. He needs every booking to arrive paid, scheduled and prepared, without the phone tag.

How might we

Turn a phone number into a four-step booking a worried family can finish on a phone, in their own language, that leaves the practice nothing to chase?

03 · Develop

Three decisions, explored as wireframes

Before polishing anything, I sketched the three choices that shaped the flow: how to ask, how records arrive, and how money moves. Several shipped as working versions first, and the client’s feedback decided which stayed.

Why pay before picking a time

Payment comes before the calendar, so every slot in the surgeon’s week belongs to someone who has already paid.

The trade-off: a transfer can’t confirm itself. The assistant matches each one to the patient’s name, which the form asks people to use as the reference.

Decision 1

How do we ask? For a relative on a phone, answering about someone else.

Cut after testingA · Long formNine labelled fields on one page. It read like a hospital form, and ran long on a phone.
ShippedB · One question per screenSix questions about the patient, with a counter, a progress line, letter keys and Enter to continue.
Kept as a side doorC · WhatsApp onlyA pre-filled message to the assistant, on every screen for people who’d rather talk.
Decision 2

How do records arrive? Before the call, not during it.

Cut after testingA · Upload in the formA drop zone per file. CT and PET-CT files are too large for a web form.
Cut after testingB · E-Nabız loginA portal password typed into a form and stored in a sheet. The client preferred to ask himself.
ShippedC · WhatsApp, after bookingOne clear instruction and a list of what to send, plus a 24-hour minimum to read it all.
Decision 3

How does money move? Upfront, into a Turkish account, with no payments integration.

Not builtA · Card checkoutInstant, but a provider means fees, set-up and verification before the first booking.
ShippedB · Bank transfer, then confirmFee, inclusions and IBAN on one screen. A short check stops anyone booking before they’ve paid.

Every screen reads in the same order

Where am I, what is being asked, how do I answer, what do I press. And every screen has to hold both Turkish and English without breaking.

  1. 1Where am IFour named steps. Done steps turn gold with a tick; the current one is navy.
  2. 2How far into this stepA 3px gold line across the top and a “5 of 6” counter. Quiet, but always there.
  3. 3The way backBack sits above every question, in the same place, so a wrong answer costs one tap.
  4. 4What is being askedThe largest thing on screen: Playfair 600, 24 to 38px, in a column no wider than 660px.
  5. 5How to answerBig targets: yes and no as two cards, choices as full-width rows with letter keys.
  6. 6What to pressOne navy button per screen, with “or press Enter” beside it for keyboards.

Design system

Tidied from the live stylesheet

23 font sizes became 14 named styles, 64 hard-coded colours became 22 tokens, and six pairings that failed WCAG AA were fixed. Documenting the parts as components is the first step to moving the code onto shared tokens.

The surgeon’s voice, and a calm interface

Playfair Display carries the surgeon’s voice: questions, headings and the numbers that matter. DM Sans carries everything people read and tap. Both draw ç, ğ, ı, İ, ö, ş and ü in every weight used.
