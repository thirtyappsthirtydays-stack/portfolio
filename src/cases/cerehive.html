What I learned

Insight 1

The role is the unit of work

Recruiters think in open roles, not candidate lists or features. The prototype made them start from everyone, then filter down to the role they cared about.

Insight 2

A score without a reason gets ignored

When the AI ranked someone 92, recruiters reread the CV to check it. Without reasons, the ranking saved little time and was hard to defend to a hiring manager.

Insight 3

One person, three records

The client’s ATS, our talent pool and job boards like Indeed each held part of the picture, so the same candidate could appear more than once.

Insight 4

The work leaves after the shortlist

Outreach moved to email, scheduling to calendars and feedback to spreadsheets. Each move lost the context of why that person was a match.

02 · Define

Designing for Priya

I brought the research together into one primary persona, then turned her week into a problem statement and four principles to test every design against.

Problem statement

Priya needs to find the best people for each open role quickly, and explain why they are the best, because her credibility with hiring managers rests on the shortlist.

How might we

Make the role the home of the work, and make every match explain itself?

Proto-personaPriya Shah, 34Talent Acquisition Lead · 350-person fintech · London

“Don’t just tell me who. Tell me why, so I can defend it to the hiring manager.”

Open roles

Measured on

Works in

Goals

  • A shortlist the hiring manager trusts within a week
  • Time spent talking to people, not reading CVs
  • Every role moving, without a spreadsheet on the side

Frustrations

  • Scores she can’t explain to a hiring manager
  • Rebuilding the same context in four tools
  • Chasing interviewers for feedback

Four principles every design was tested against

01

The role is home

Every task starts from a role and comes back to it: matches, outreach, interviews and the brief.

02

Every score shows its reasons

No number appears without the evidence behind it, written in plain language.

03

The recruiter has the last word

Recruiters confirm or correct the AI. Nobody is rejected automatically.

04

One person, one profile

Candidates from every source merge into a single record, with each source still visible.

Design system

Purple acts, gold explains

14 text styles, 3 ramps of 10 colours, 9 spacing steps, 4 radii and 3 elevations. Every style is a Figma style and a code token with the same name, and it became the base every later feature was built on.

One typeface, one scale, numbers that line up

Recruiters scan long, dense lists, so the type had two jobs: keep names and reasons readable at small sizes, and make scores easy to compare.

Share of a typical screen
~70% white, 14% greys, 8% ink, 6% purple, 2% gold. A target, not a measurement: it keeps gold rare enough that the eye goes straight to the scores.
A 4px grid for everything

Every space, size and line height is a multiple of 4, so screens built by different people still line up, and engineers never have to guess a value.
