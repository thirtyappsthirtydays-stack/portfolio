What I learned

Insight 1

The role is the unit of work

The prototype started from all candidates, then filtered down to a role.

Insight 2

A score without a reason gets ignored

When the AI ranked someone 92, recruiters reread the CV to check it.

Insight 3

The same candidate in several systems

The ATS, our talent pool and Indeed each held part of the picture.

Insight 4

Work moves to other tools after the shortlist

Outreach, scheduling and feedback moved to email, calendars and spreadsheets, and the context of each match was lost.

02 · Define

Designing for Priya

I combined the research into one persona: the talent lead who owns the shortlist.

Proto-personaPriya Shah, 34Talent Acquisition Lead · 350-person fintech · London

“Show me why someone is a match, so I can explain it to the hiring manager.”

Goals

  • A shortlist the hiring manager trusts within a week
  • More time talking to candidates

Frustrations

  • Scores she can’t explain to a hiring manager
  • Rebuilding the same context in four tools

Proto-persona from research synthesis. Stock photo, not a real participant.

Problem statement

Priya needs to find the best people for each open role quickly, and explain why they are the best, because her credibility with hiring managers rests on the shortlist.

How might we

Make the role the home of the work, and make every match explain itself?

Four principles

01

The role is home

Every task starts from a role and comes back to it.

02

Every score shows its reasons

No number appears without the evidence behind it.

03

The recruiter has the last word

Recruiters confirm or correct the AI. Nobody is rejected automatically.

04

Merge duplicate candidates

One record per person, with every source still visible.
